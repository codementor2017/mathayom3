🛡️ Lesson 9 — Responding to Misinformation

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 3 🗓️ Semester 2 · Midterm 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"Your aunt shares a false health claim in the family group chat. Your classmate shares a conspiracy video. Your friend forwards a rumor. What do you do? Correcting them badly can push them further into the false claim."

In this lesson, you will learn how to respond to misinformation with respect and evidence. You will practice the truth sandwich technique, role-play corrections, and build habits that help stop misinformation from spreading.

Image 9.1 — Lesson Opener Description: A warm parchment banner showing a family group chat with a false health claim message. Beside the chat, a student holds a small scroll labeled "Truth Sandwich." A green checkmark floats over the correct response. Warm bronze tones.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Apply the seven strategies for responding to misinformation.
  • Use the truth sandwich method to correct false claims.
  • Correct misinformation respectfully and effectively.
  • Refuse to spread misinformation yourself.

📖 1. Seven Strategies for Responding

When you encounter misinformation, follow these seven steps.

  1. Pause. Do not react immediately. Give yourself time to think.
  2. Verify. Check the facts using reliable sources before responding.
  3. Respond respectfully. Do not attack the person. Attack the claim with evidence.
  4. Provide evidence. Share links to reliable sources.
  5. Correct gently. Use phrases like: "I understand why you shared this, but I found..."
  6. Do not spread it. Never forward the false information, not even to correct it.
  7. Report harmful content. Use platform tools if the content violates rules or could cause harm.
Tip: You are more likely to convince someone when you are calm and respectful. Anger pushes people away, even when you are right.

Activity 1 — What Would You Do? 8 min

For each scenario, write which of the seven strategies you would use first and why.

  1. A friend shares a fake news article with you privately.
  2. A family member posts a false health claim in a public group chat.
  3. A classmate shares a conspiracy theory video.
  4. You see a rumor spreading in a group chat.

Compare with a partner. Which scenario would be the hardest to handle?

📖 2. The Truth Sandwich

When you correct misinformation, order matters. If you start by repeating the false claim, you may accidentally reinforce it. Instead, use the truth sandwich:

  1. Start with the truth. State the correct fact clearly.
  2. Mention the false claim. Briefly explain that a false claim has been going around — but don't repeat it in detail.
  3. End with the truth again. Restate the correct information to leave that in the reader's mind.
Example:
"Vaccines are safe. Some people say they cause autism, but this has been disproven by many studies. Vaccines are safe and save lives."

Notice how the truth appears at the start AND the end. The false claim is mentioned only briefly in the middle.

Activity 2 — Build a Truth Sandwich 10 min

Choose three false claims from the list below. For each, write a truth sandwich response.

  • "Drinking hot water kills viruses."
  • "The moon landing was faked."
  • "5G causes illness."
  • "Vaccines cause autism."
  • "A celebrity was arrested for something they didn't do."

Share one response with a partner. Did your truth sandwich feel natural?

📖 3. Correcting Respectfully — Real Conversations

Correcting someone is a delicate task. Here are some sentence starters that help.

  • "I used to think that too, but then I found this..."
  • "I understand why this seems convincing, but I checked and..."
  • "That's an interesting point. Let's look at the data together."
  • "I found a source that says the opposite. Would you be willing to look at it?"

The goal is not to "win" the argument. The goal is to help someone see the truth. Being right is useless if the other person refuses to listen.

Key idea: Correcting misinformation is a responsibility — but it must be done with respect.

Activity 3 — Write a Respectful Response 10 min

For each scenario, write a respectful response using the truth sandwich and one of the sentence starters above.

  1. A friend shares a fake news article on their story.
  2. A family member forwards a false health claim.
  3. A classmate shares a conspiracy theory in the group chat.
  4. A neighbor posts an unverified rumor about a local business.

Read one of your responses aloud to the class. Discuss: what makes a response effective?

🔍 Case Study — A Family Group Chat

Your aunt shares a message in the family group chat: "Doctors are hiding a natural cure for cancer — big pharma doesn't want you to know!" Several family members reply with concern. What do you do?

Applying the strategies:

  • Pause. Don't reply immediately with anger.
  • Verify. Check cancer.org, WHO, or a university health site.
  • Respond respectfully. "I understand the concern. I looked this up and found that..."
  • Provide evidence. Share one or two reliable links.
  • Correct gently. Use the truth sandwich.
  • Do not spread it. Don't share the original message.
  • Report if needed. If the message encourages dangerous actions, report to the platform.

Example response:

"I understand why this seems worrying, Auntie. I looked it up and the World Health Organization says there is no hidden cure. Cancer treatment is based on decades of research. If you want, I can send you a link to a doctor-reviewed page. I know we all want the best for our family."

Reflect: How is this response different from just saying "That's fake"?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Role-Play Correction

Working in pairs, role-play the following scenarios. One person shares misinformation; the other responds using the seven strategies and the truth sandwich.

  1. A friend shares a fake news article about a celebrity.
  2. A family member shares a false health claim.
  3. A classmate shares a conspiracy theory.
  4. A neighbor posts an unverified rumor about a local business.

Switch roles for each scenario. After each role-play, give each other feedback:

  • Was the response respectful?
  • Did it use the truth sandwich?
  • Was the evidence clear?

Then perform one role-play for the class. The class will give feedback.

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. What are the seven strategies for responding to misinformation?
  2. What is the truth sandwich? Why does the order matter?
  3. Why is it important to correct misinformation respectfully?
  4. What did you learn from the role-play activity?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Seven strategies: pause, verify, respond respectfully, provide evidence, correct gently, don't spread, report.
  • The truth sandwich places the truth first and last, with the false claim only briefly in the middle.
  • Correct misinformation with respect, not anger.
  • Correcting misinformation is a civic responsibility.
