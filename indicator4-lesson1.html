💳 Lesson 1 — Safe Online Transactions

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"Have you or your family ever bought something online? How did you pay? Was it safe? Did you ever wonder how you know the seller is real?"

Online shopping is convenient — but it also comes with risks. Every year, people lose millions of baht to fake websites, phishing scams, and stolen credit card numbers. In this lesson, you will learn to recognize the types of online transactions, the risks involved, and the safety practices that protect you.

📖 1. Types of Online Transactions

Online transactions are any exchanges of money that happen over the internet. There are five main types.

1) E-Commerce

Buying physical goods from online stores. Examples: Shopee, Lazada, Amazon.

2) Digital Goods

Buying apps, games, music, movies, or e-books. You receive a file or a license, not a physical object.

3) Subscriptions

Paying a recurring fee for a service. Examples: Netflix, Spotify, YouTube Premium.

4) In-Game Purchases

Buying items, currency, or upgrades inside a game. This includes skins, loot boxes, and battle passes.

5) Bill Payments

Paying electricity, water, internet, or phone bills through an app or a bank website.

Key idea: Every type of transaction carries its own risks. The safer you are at recognizing them, the less likely you are to be scammed.

📖 2. Common Risks in Online Transactions

Every type of transaction has risks. Here are the most common ones.

Fake Websites

Scammers copy real websites and change the URL slightly (e.g., "shopee-th.com" instead of "shopee.co.th"). They look real but steal your payment details.

Stolen Credit Card Numbers

If your payment information is stolen, scammers can use it to buy things in your name.

Phishing Payment Pages

Fake payment pages that look official but send your money to a scammer. These often arrive by email or SMS.

No Delivery After Payment

The seller takes your money but never ships the product. Common on social media marketplaces.

Hidden Fees

Extra charges added at checkout (shipping, taxes, processing fees) that were not shown earlier.

Tip: If a deal looks too good to be true, it usually is. A 90%-off offer from an unknown seller is a big red flag.

📖 3. Seven Safety Practices

Follow these seven practices every time you shop or pay online.

  1. Use official apps or websites. Download from the official app store. Type the URL carefully.
  2. Check for HTTPS. Look for the padlock icon in your browser. It means the connection is encrypted.
  3. Use secure payment methods. Credit cards and official payment platforms offer buyer protection.
  4. Never share OTP codes. No legitimate seller will ever ask for your OTP.
  5. Keep receipts. Save confirmation emails and screenshots.
  6. Check reviews before buying. Look for reviews from real customers, not just 5-star bots.
  7. Use a separate card for online shopping. If it gets stolen, only that card is affected.
Example: Before paying for an online order, ask yourself:
  • Is the website official?
  • Does it have HTTPS?
  • Am I using a secure payment method?
  • Have I saved the receipt?
If all four are yes, you are probably safe.

Activity 3 — Evaluate the Scenario 10 min

Read each scenario and answer:

  1. Is this transaction safe? Why or why not?
  2. What safety practice is being ignored?
  3. What should the buyer do?

Scenarios:

  • A student buys a game from an unofficial site offering it at half price.
  • A shopper pays using a credit card on an official website with HTTPS.
  • A customer receives an SMS asking for OTP to "confirm a purchase."
  • A buyer sends money via direct transfer to a seller they found on social media.

🔍 Case Study — The Phishing SMS

A Grade 9 student received an SMS: "Your order was delayed. Click here to confirm your payment." The message included a link.

What happened: The student clicked the link. It opened a page that looked like the official bank site, asking for the username, password, and OTP.

What went wrong:

  • The SMS was a phishing message — the sender was not the real bank.
  • The link led to a fake page that copied the bank's design.
  • The page asked for an OTP — a definite red flag. Real banks never ask for OTP this way.

What should have been done:

  • Never click links in unsolicited SMS messages.
  • Type the official bank URL directly in a browser.
  • Never share your OTP with anyone.
  • Report the message to the bank and delete it.

Reflect: What would you have done if you had received this SMS?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Create a Safe Transaction Checklist

Working in pairs, create a "Safe Online Transaction Checklist" for your classmates. Your checklist should have three parts:

  1. Before buying: What should you check?
  2. During payment: What should you look for?
  3. After purchase: What should you keep or do?

Include at least 5 items in each section. Use clear, direct language that a younger student could understand.

When finished, exchange checklists with another pair. Give each other feedback on clarity and completeness.

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Five types of transactions: e-commerce, digital goods, subscriptions, in-game purchases, bill payments.
  • Common risks: fake websites, stolen cards, phishing, no delivery, hidden fees.
  • Seven safety practices protect you: official sites, HTTPS, secure payment, no OTP sharing, receipts, reviews, separate card.
  • When in doubt, pause and verify.
