⚖️ Lesson 4 — Computer Laws in Thailand

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"Do the laws that apply in the real world also apply online? If you hack a school account, share someone's private data, or post false information about a business — can you be punished?"

Yes. In Thailand, several important laws govern how we use computers and the internet. In this lesson, you will learn the three most important ones: the Computer Crime Act, the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), and the Copyright Act. You will see how these laws apply to real situations and understand the consequences of breaking them.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Identify three key Thai computer laws.
  • Explain the consequences of violating these laws.
  • Apply the laws to real scenarios.
  • Understand why ignorance of the law is not an excuse.

📖 1. The Computer Crime Act

The Computer Crime Act (พระราชบัญญัติว่าด้วยการกระทำความผิดเกี่ยวกับคอมพิวเตอร์) is Thailand's main law against cybercrime. It covers five main areas:

  • Illegal access — accessing a computer system without permission (hacking).
  • Data interception — secretly reading or copying data that is not yours.
  • Data modification — changing or deleting data without authorization.
  • Spreading false computer data — posting false information that harms others or the public.
  • Distributing illegal content — sharing content that breaks other laws.

Penalties can include fines and imprisonment. The exact penalty depends on the crime.

Key idea: Hacking is not a game. Even "just looking" at someone's private account without permission is a crime.

Activity 1 — Which Area of the Law? 8 min

For each scenario, write which area of the Computer Crime Act applies.

  1. Logging into a friend's email account without their permission.
  2. Posting a false review that damages a business's reputation.
  3. Copying a company's private files from a shared drive.
  4. Deleting files from a school computer you don't own.
  5. Sharing illegal content online.

Compare with a partner.

📖 2. The Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA)

The PDPA (พระราชบัญญัติคุ้มครองข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล) protects your personal data. It gives you rights and places duties on anyone who collects your data.

What Counts as Personal Data

  • Name, address, phone number, email.
  • ID card number, passport number.
  • Health data, financial data, biometric data.
  • Online identifiers (IP address, location).

Your Rights Under the PDPA

  • To know how your data is being used.
  • To give consent before your data is collected.
  • To access your own data.
  • To ask for corrections or deletion.

Rules for Organizations

Any organization that collects personal data must ask for consent, protect the data, and use it only for the purpose stated.

Example: A school app cannot ask for your home address unless it explains why. If the school wants to share your data with a third party, they must ask for your permission first.

Activity 2 — Apply the PDPA 10 min

For each scenario, decide whether the PDPA has been violated. Explain your reasoning.

  1. A friend posts your photo without your permission, showing your house number.
  2. A shopping app asks for your ID card number to send a T-shirt.
  3. A school app collects data on student attendance without saying why.
  4. A restaurant takes your phone number "for marketing" without asking.
  5. Someone shares a screenshot of your chat messages.

Share your analysis with the class.

📖 3. The Copyright Act

The Copyright Act (พระราชบัญญัติลิขสิทธิ์) protects the work of creators — songs, books, images, software, videos, and more.

What Is Protected

  • Books, articles, and written works.
  • Music and songs.
  • Films and videos.
  • Photographs and artwork.
  • Software and code.

What Infringes Copyright

  • Downloading or sharing copyrighted content without permission.
  • Selling copies of someone else's work.
  • Using someone else's work in your own without permission or attribution.
  • Publicly performing someone's song without a license.

There are some legal exceptions, especially for fair use in education and commentary. You will learn more about fair use in Lesson 5.

Tip: If you want to use someone else's work, look for:
  • Creative Commons licenses (which allow reuse with conditions).
  • Public domain works (which are free to use).
  • The original creator's permission.

🔍 Case Study — The Shared Password

A Grade 9 student named Beam used a shared password given to him by a friend to log into the friend's school account. Beam was curious and looked around, changed nothing, and logged out.

Was it illegal? Yes. Even though Beam did not change anything, he accessed a computer system without permission. This is a violation of the Computer Crime Act. Penalties can include a fine or even imprisonment depending on the case.

What could have happened if the school reported it?

  • Beam could have been investigated.
  • He could have faced school discipline and legal action.
  • His friend (who shared the password) could also have been punished.

Lesson: Never log in to someone else's account — even if they give you permission. It is a violation of computer law.

Reflect: Have you ever been tempted to look at something you were not supposed to? What stopped you? What could have happened?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Case Study Analysis

Working in groups of 3–4, choose one of the case studies below (or one provided by your teacher). Analyze it using the following questions:

  1. What happened? Summarize the case.
  2. What law was broken? (Computer Crime Act, PDPA, or Copyright Act.)
  3. What are the consequences? Possible fines or imprisonment?
  4. What should have been done instead?
  5. How can this be prevented?

Case study options:

  • A student posts a rumor about another student that damages their reputation.
  • A user downloads pirated software from a torrent site.
  • A company collects personal data without consent.
  • A gamer uses cheats to modify game files and share them.

Present your case study to the class (4 minutes per group).

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • The Computer Crime Act covers hacking, data interception, false data, and illegal content.
  • The PDPA protects your personal data and requires consent for its collection.
  • The Copyright Act protects creative works.
  • Ignorance of the law is not an excuse — you are responsible for your online actions.
