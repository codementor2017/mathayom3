⚙️ Lesson 6 — IoT Sensors and Actuators

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 1 🗓️ Semester 1 · Midterm 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"How does your phone know to rotate the screen when you turn it sideways? How does a smart doorbell know someone is at the door? How does a greenhouse know when to water its plants?"

Behind every smart device is a pair of tiny heroes: a sensor that notices something in the world, and an actuator that does something about it. In this lesson, you will meet the most common sensors and actuators, learn what each one does, and match them to real problems.

📖 1. Sensors — The Eyes and Ears of IoT

A sensor is a device that measures something in the physical world and turns it into data that a computer can read. Think of sensors as the "senses" of a smart system — just like your eyes, ears, and skin tell your brain what is happening around you.

Six Common Sensors

  • Temperature sensor — measures heat. Used in thermostats, weather stations, and cooking appliances.
  • Humidity sensor — measures moisture in the air. Used in greenhouses, weather apps, and drying machines.
  • Light sensor — measures brightness. Used in phone screens that dim automatically and streetlights that turn on at night.
  • Motion sensor — detects movement. Used in security alarms and automatic doors.
  • Soil moisture sensor — measures water in soil. Used in smart farms and automatic irrigation systems.
  • Ultrasonic sensor — measures distance using sound waves. Used in car parking sensors and robots.
Key idea: Sensors do not "act" — they only observe. They turn physical conditions into data.

📖 2. Actuators — The Hands of IoT

An actuator is a device that takes action. It receives a signal from the system and does something physical — moves, lights up, makes a sound, or controls another device.

Five Common Actuators

  • LED — a small light that turns on and off. Used as indicators on devices, displays, and smart lights.
  • Buzzer — produces sound. Used in alarms, notifications, and timers.
  • Motor — produces continuous spinning motion. Used in fans, robots, and conveyor belts.
  • Servo — produces precise, controlled movement (like a small arm). Used in robot arms, camera gimbals, and steering systems.
  • Relay — an electrical switch that turns high-power devices on or off. Used in smart plugs, irrigation pumps, and industrial machines.
Example: A smart fan uses a temperature sensor to detect heat and a motor (actuator) to spin the blades when it gets too warm.

📖 3. Sensors + Actuators = Smart Systems

Sensors alone only gather data. Actuators alone only react. When combined, they form the heart of every smart system.

Here are three examples that combine both:

System Sensor Actuator What It Does
Smart Fan Temperature sensor Motor Turns on the fan when it's hot
Smart Garden Soil moisture sensor Relay + water pump Waters plants when soil is dry
Smart Doorbell Motion sensor + camera Buzzer + LED Rings and flashes when someone approaches
Tip: When designing a smart system, always ask: "What should the sensor detect?" and "What should the actuator do about it?" Once you can answer those two questions, you have the core of your design.

Activity 3 — Design a Sensor-Actuator System 10 min

Pick one problem below and design a mini system.

  • A smart plant pot that waters itself.
  • A smart classroom that adjusts its lights.
  • A smart fan that turns on when it's hot.
  • A smart trash bin that alerts when it's full.

In your notebook, write:

  1. Sensor(s): What will it measure?
  2. Actuator(s): What will it do?

🔍 Case Study — How a Smart Greenhouse Works

A smart greenhouse in Chiang Rai grows orchids. Orchids need exactly the right temperature, humidity, and light. Here is how the greenhouse's IoT system keeps them happy, 24 hours a day.

Sensors:

  • Temperature sensor — reads the air temperature every minute.
  • Humidity sensor — reads the moisture in the air.
  • Light sensor — measures how bright the sunlight is.
  • Soil moisture sensor — checks how wet the roots are.

Processing rules:

  • If temperature > 32°C → turn on the fans.
  • If humidity < 50% → turn on the mist sprayer.
  • If light < 30% of ideal → turn on the grow lamps.
  • If soil moisture < 40% → open the drip irrigation valve.

Actuators: fans (motors), mist sprayer (relay + pump), grow lamps (relay), drip valve (servo).

User interface: The gardener sees a live dashboard on her phone showing temperature, humidity, light, and moisture. She gets a daily summary and an alert only when something goes wrong.

Result: The orchids bloomed on time, and the gardener checked her phone instead of walking the greenhouse every hour.

Reflect: What sensors would you add to this greenhouse to make it even smarter? What actuators could it use for pest control?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Design Your Own Smart System

In pairs, choose a scenario and design a full sensor-actuator system.

Choose one:

  • A smart bed that adjusts its firmness.
  • A smart shoe rack that dries wet shoes.
  • A smart backpack that warns you if you forgot something.
  • A smart pet feeder that gives food on a schedule and when the pet is hungry.

Design the following on a large sheet of paper:

  1. Sensor(s) and what they measure.
  2. Processing rules (using if / else).
  3. Actuator(s) and what they do.
  4. User interface (how the user sees what's happening).
  5. One risk (what could go wrong with the system).

When finished, present your design to another pair. Give each other feedback using the phrase: "One thing I like is... One thing I would improve is..."

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Sensors observe the world and turn physical data into digital signals.
  • Actuators take action based on those signals.
  • Common sensors: temperature, humidity, light, motion, soil moisture, ultrasonic.
  • Common actuators: LED, buzzer, motor, servo, relay.
  • Smart systems combine sensors + processing + actuators + a user interface.
