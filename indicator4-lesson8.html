📱 Lesson 8 — Responsible Social Media Use

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"How much time do you spend on social media each day? What do you post about? Do you ever regret something you posted? Your posts live online forever — and they shape how others see you."

Social media is a powerful tool for connection, creativity, and learning. But it also has risks. In this lesson, you will learn five responsibilities for using social media well, and you will create your own personal social media policy.

Image 8.1 — Lesson Opener Description: A warm parchment banner showing a student holding a phone with social media icons (like, comment, share) floating around. A small clock shows "3 hours." The student's expression shows both engagement and reflection. Warm bronze tones.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Explain what responsible social media use means.
  • Identify the five responsibilities of social media use.
  • Apply the audience test to your posts.
  • Create your own personal social media policy.

📖 1. The Five Responsibilities of Social Media Use

Every social media user has five responsibilities.

1) Think Before Posting

Consider the impact of what you are about to share. Ask: Would I want this shared about me?

2) Respect Others

Do not post about others without their consent. Do not share photos, messages, or personal information about someone else without permission.

3) Protect Privacy

Do not share your address, ID number, phone number, or any personal data publicly.

4) Verify Before Sharing

Do not spread misinformation. Use the five-step verification and share test from Lesson 7.

5) Be Kind

Do not cyberbully, troll, or harass others. Digital cruelty is still cruelty — and it can be illegal.

Key idea: Social media is a public space. What you post becomes part of your digital footprint — and it can affect your future.
Image 8.2 — Five Responsibilities Description: Five parchment cards in a row. Each card has a responsibility and an icon: Think (a brain), Respect (a handshake), Privacy (a lock), Verify (a magnifier), Be Kind (a heart). Warm bronze borders.

Activity 1 — Which Responsibility? 8 min

For each scenario, name the responsibility that is being ignored.

  1. Posting a friend's photo without asking.
  2. Sharing an unverified news article.
  3. Writing an angry post about someone.
  4. Posting your home address in an "About Me" section.
  5. Commenting on a friend's post with insults.

📖 2. The Audience Test

Before posting, imagine the following three audiences seeing your post.

  1. Your parents. Would you be comfortable if your parents saw this?
  2. Your teachers. Would you be comfortable if your teachers saw this?
  3. A future employer. Would you be comfortable if someone hiring you saw this?

If the answer to all three is yes, the post is probably safe. If any answer is no, reconsider.

Example: You want to post a picture of yourself at a party with friends. Would your parents be okay? Probably. Would your teachers care? Probably not. Would a future employer? Depends on the picture. If it is embarrassing, wait or do not post it.
Image 8.3 — The Audience Test Description: A parchment showing a phone screen with a post. Around the phone, three figures look at the screen: a parent, a teacher, and a future employer in a suit. Small speech bubbles show their possible reactions. Warm bronze tones.

Activity 2 — Analyze Social Media Posts 10 min

For each post, answer:

  1. Is this responsible?
  2. What is the impact?
  3. Would you post this?
  4. What would you change?

Posts:

  • A post complaining about a teacher by name.
  • A photo of a friend without permission.
  • A rant about a bad day at school.
  • A post celebrating a friend's success.
  • A post sharing an unverified news article.

Discuss with the class. Which posts need the most changes?

📖 3. Your Digital Footprint

Your digital footprint is the trail you leave online — every post, comment, like, share, and search. It is permanent. Even deleted posts can be recovered.

Who might look at your digital footprint?

  • Future schools and universities.
  • Future employers.
  • Friends and family.
  • Journalists (in some cases).

A positive digital footprint can help you — for example, showing your achievements, creative work, or volunteer activity. A negative digital footprint can harm you for years.

Tip: Treat your social media as a portfolio — a place that shows who you are at your best. Post things you will be proud of in five years.
Image 8.4 — Digital Footprint Description: A parchment showing a trail of small footprints leading from a phone into the distance. Along the trail, small icons: a post, a comment, a like, a photo, a share. Warm bronze tones.

Activity 3 — Create Your Personal Social Media Policy 10 min

In your notebook, write a short "Personal Social Media Policy." Include:

  1. What I will post: (3 rules)
  2. What I will not post: (3 rules)
  3. How I will treat others: (2 rules)
  4. How I will protect my privacy: (2 rules)
  5. How I will verify before sharing: (2 rules)
  6. My screen time goal: ______ hours per day

Share your policy with a partner. Which rule is the hardest for you to follow?

🔍 Case Study — The Post That Cost a Scholarship

A high school student named Anan applied for a university scholarship. He had good grades and strong recommendations. But when the scholarship committee checked his social media, they found several posts where he insulted classmates and shared unverified political rumors.

Result: Anan did not receive the scholarship. The committee said his online behavior did not reflect the values they were looking for.

What was wrong:

  • The posts were public.
  • They showed disrespect to others.
  • They included misinformation.
  • They contradicted his application materials.

What should have been done:

  • Think before posting.
  • Use the audience test.
  • Treat others with respect online.
  • Use social media to showcase positive achievements instead.

Reflect: Would you want someone to judge you based only on your social media? What would they see?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Review Your Own Posts

Working individually, review your own social media posts from the past month (or imagine what you typically post). For each post, ask:

  1. Would I post this again today?
  2. Would my parents be comfortable seeing it?
  3. Would a future employer?
  4. Does it reflect who I want to be?

Then write a one-paragraph reflection (6–8 sentences):

  • What did you find when you reviewed your posts?
  • What would you change?
  • What will you do differently from now on?

Share one insight from your reflection with a partner.

Image 8.6 — Review Template Description: A parchment template with four questions and a reflection section. A small mirror icon at the top. Warm parchment tones.

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. Name the five responsibilities of social media use.
  2. What is the audience test?
  3. What is a digital footprint, and why does it matter?
  4. What is one rule you will add to your personal social media policy?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Five responsibilities: think, respect, protect privacy, verify, be kind.
  • The audience test checks if your post is appropriate for parents, teachers, and future employers.
  • Your digital footprint is permanent and can affect your future.
  • Treat social media as a positive portfolio of who you are.

🏠 Homework

Continue to review your social media posts. Write a short paragraph (5–7 sentences) reflecting on what you found and how you will improve your online presence.
