🏆 Lesson 10 — Final Project: App or IoT System

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 1 🗓️ Semester 1 · Midterm 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"You have learned how apps are built, how they look, how they think, and how they connect to the world. Now it is your turn. Today, you become the creator."

In this final lesson for Indicator 1, you will design and build a complete app or IoT system that integrates with another subject. You will apply everything from Lessons 1–9: the 6-step process, UI design, variables, conditions, functions, and (if you choose) sensors and actuators. At the end, you will present your work to the class.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Apply the full 6-step app development process to a real project.
  • Integrate your app or IoT system with another school subject.
  • Document your work with requirements, design, code, and test cases.
  • Present and justify your project to the class.
  • Reflect on what you learned and how you would improve.

📖 1. Your Project Task

Your task is to design and build one of the following:

  • An app that integrates with another school subject (Math, Science, Language, Social Studies, Health, or Art).
  • An IoT system that uses sensors and actuators to solve a real-world problem.

Your project must include:

  1. A clear problem statement — what problem does it solve?
  2. Subject integration — how does it connect to another subject?
  3. All 6 steps documented: requirements, design, prototype, develop, test, deploy.
  4. Variables, conditions, and functions (for apps) or sensors, connectivity, and actuators (for IoT).
  5. A UI sketch showing the layout.
  6. 3 test cases — including at least one edge case.
  7. A 5-minute presentation to the class.
  8. A short written reflection.
Tip: Choose a topic you actually care about. The best projects solve a problem that matters to the person who builds them.

Activity 1 — Choose Your Topic 10 min

In your notebook, write down your choice for the following:

  1. Type: App or IoT system?
  2. Subject integration: Which school subject will you integrate with?
  3. Problem: What problem does your project solve?
  4. User: Who will use your project?
  5. Why you chose it: One sentence explaining why this project matters to you.

Share your plan with the teacher or a partner. Get feedback before starting.

📖 2. Project Planning

Before you build, write a full plan. Use the structure below.

Step 1 — Requirements

  • Problem statement (1–2 sentences).
  • User description.
  • Features the app or system must have.

Step 2 — Design

  • Sketch the main screen (for apps) or the system layout (for IoT).
  • Label all elements.
  • Show the user flow (what happens when the user taps or does something).

Step 3 — Prototype

  • Describe or draw a simple version of your project.
  • Explain how you will test the design before coding.

Step 4 — Develop

  • List variables, conditions, and functions (for apps).
  • List sensors, connectivity, and actuators (for IoT).
  • Write or draw the code blocks.

Step 5 — Test

  • Write three test cases with inputs and expected outputs.
  • Include at least one edge case (e.g., zero input, negative number, empty field).

Step 6 — Deploy and Reflect

  • How will you share your project? (Show it to classmates, publish it, etc.)
  • What would you improve if you had more time?

Activity 2 — Write Your Plan 15 min

In your notebook, complete the six steps of your project plan. Be specific.

Example plan for a BMI Calculator app:

  1. Requirements: accepts weight and height; computes BMI; shows category; works in under 5 seconds.
  2. Design: one screen, title, two inputs, one button, one output.
  3. Prototype: sketch on paper and test with 3 classmates.
  4. Develop: variables (weight, height, bmi); conditions (if bmi < 18.5, etc.); functions (calculate_bmi, get_category).
  5. Test: (60, 1.65) → 22.0 Normal; (45, 1.65) → 16.5 Underweight; (0, 0) → error.
  6. Deploy: share QR code at health fair; collect feedback.

Show your plan to your teacher before moving on to the build phase.

📖 3. Building Your Project

Now it is time to build. Use your plan as a guide, but stay flexible — you may find that you need to change something along the way. That is normal.

If you are building an app:

  • Open AppInventor, Python, or Scratch.
  • Build the interface first (text boxes, buttons, labels).
  • Add the logic (variables, conditions, functions).
  • Test with your three test cases.

If you are building an IoT system:

  • Sketch the system layout (sensors, connectivity, processing, actuators).
  • Write the processing rules (if / else statements).
  • Describe the actuators and how they respond.
  • Explain how the user receives data.

Keep your plan on your desk. Refer to it often. If you get stuck, ask your teacher or a classmate for help — but try your own solution first.

Time check: You have about 25 minutes to build. Use your time wisely. Focus on making the core features work before adding extras.

🔍 Case Study — A Great Project from Last Year

Last year, a Grade 9 student named Mali built an app that helped her classmates study Thai tone marks. Here is how she approached it:

Problem: Many students struggled to remember which tone mark went with which tone.

Subject integration: Language (Thai).

Requirements:

  • Take a tone mark as input (none, ่, ้, ๊, ๋).
  • Take consonant class and syllable type as inputs.
  • Return the correct tone with a short explanation.

Design: One screen with four dropdowns (tone mark, consonant class, vowel length, final sound) and one big "Show Tone" button.

Test cases:

  • Mid-class, live, no mark → Mid (passed).
  • Mid-class, live, mai ek → Low (passed).
  • Empty input → error message (passed).

Result: Her classmates used the app to practice before exams, and her test scores improved by 15%.

Reflect: What made Mali's project strong? (Hint: it solved a real problem for real users.)

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Prepare Your Presentation

You have about 15 minutes to prepare a 5-minute presentation. Your presentation must include:

  1. Problem statement — what problem does your project solve?
  2. Subject integration — how does it connect to another subject?
  3. 6-step process — show how you moved from idea to project.
  4. Demo — show the app or IoT system in action.
  5. Test results — how did your three test cases go?
  6. What you learned — one sentence about your biggest takeaway.

Practice your presentation with a partner. Give each other feedback on clarity and time.

📝 Presentation & Reflection

Each student or group presents for 5 minutes. After all presentations, write a short reflection in your notebook:

  1. What was the hardest part of your project?
  2. How did integrating another subject make your project more meaningful?
  3. What would you improve if you had one more week?
  4. What did you learn from watching other students' presentations?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • The 6-step process works for every kind of project.
  • Good projects start with a real problem and a real user.
  • Testing with edge cases makes a project reliable.
  • Presenting your work helps you see what you learned and what you would do differently.
