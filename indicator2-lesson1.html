📊 Lesson 1 — Primary and Secondary Data Sources

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 2 🗓️ Semester 1 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"You want to know the average temperature in Bangkok last year. Where do you look? Do you measure it yourself for a whole year? Or do you find an official weather report?"

Every decision we make is based on data. But not all data is collected the same way. Some data comes from our own observations, and some comes from other people's work. In this lesson, you will learn the difference between primary data and secondary data — and why smart decision-makers use both.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Define primary data and secondary data.
  • Identify reliable secondary sources.
  • Explain the advantages and disadvantages of each type.
  • Combine both types of data to make a real decision.

📖 1. What Is Primary Data?

Primary data is data you collect yourself. You are the first person to gather it, directly from the source. It comes from:

  • Surveys — asking people questions and recording their answers.
  • Interviews — talking to someone one-on-one to gather details.
  • Measurements — using tools (thermometer, ruler, scale) to record numbers.
  • Observations — watching something happen and writing down what you see.
  • Experiments — testing a hypothesis and recording the results.
Key idea: Primary data belongs to you. You control the questions, the method, and the quality.

Advantages of Primary Data

  • You control the quality and relevance.
  • You can ask follow-up questions to get more detail.
  • You know exactly when, where, and how the data was collected.

Disadvantages of Primary Data

  • It takes time and effort to collect.
  • Sample sizes are often small.
  • Your questions might accidentally bias the results.

📖 2. What Is Secondary Data?

Secondary data is data that someone else has already collected. You are not the first person to gather it — you are using work that already exists. It comes from:

  • Government reports — statistics on population, weather, health, etc.
  • Research papers — scientific studies written by experts.
  • News articles — reports on current events.
  • Databases — organized collections of data (crop prices, sports stats).
  • Books and encyclopedias — reference material.
Key idea: Secondary data saves time, but you must always check if it is reliable, current, and relevant to your question.

Advantages of Secondary Data

  • It is faster to find than collecting your own data.
  • It is often larger in scale (thousands of people, many years).
  • It may include expert analysis and context.

Disadvantages of Secondary Data

  • It may be outdated.
  • It may be biased or unreliable.
  • It may not answer your exact question.

📖 3. Combining Primary and Secondary Data

The best decisions use both types of data. Primary data tells you what is happening right here, right now. Secondary data tells you what is happening in a wider context — or over a longer time.

Example — Choosing a phone promotion:

  • Primary data: your own monthly data usage, call minutes, and budget.
  • Secondary data: published rates for three different providers.

By combining them, you can see which promotion fits your real needs.

Example — Choosing a crop for local soil:

  • Primary data: soil pH and moisture from your own garden.
  • Secondary data: government crop guides and market demand reports.

Together, they tell you which crop is both possible and profitable.

Tip: When you use secondary data, always ask: Who collected it? When? Why? Who benefits from the findings? Good researchers never trust a source blindly.

🔍 Case Study — Choosing a Phone Promotion

A Grade 9 student named Anan wanted to buy a phone plan. Three promotions were available. Here is how he used both primary and secondary data.

Primary data (Anan collected himself):

  • His average monthly data usage: 12 GB (from his phone settings).
  • His average call time: 80 minutes per month.
  • His monthly budget: 400 THB.

Secondary data (from the three providers' websites):

Promotion Price Data Calls Contract
Promo A300 THB20 GB100 min12 months
Promo B450 THBUnlimited200 min6 months
Promo C250 THB10 GB50 minNone

Anan's decision:

  • Promo B is too expensive (over budget).
  • Promo C has only 10 GB — too little for his 12 GB usage.
  • Promo A gives 20 GB and 100 minutes at 300 THB — fits his usage and budget.

Conclusion: Anan chose Promo A. Both types of data were needed: his own usage (primary) and the provider rates (secondary).

Reflect: What if Anan's usage had been 5 GB instead of 12 GB? Would his choice change? Why?

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. What is the difference between primary and secondary data?
  2. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of each type.
  3. Why is it better to use both types together?
  4. What is one decision you make regularly that could benefit from both types of data?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Primary data is collected by you (surveys, measurements, interviews).
  • Secondary data is collected by someone else (reports, papers, databases).
  • Primary data is controllable but slow; secondary data is fast but must be checked for reliability.
  • The best decisions combine both types of data.
