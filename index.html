Term 1 — Introduction to App Development & IoT

In this opening term, students step into the world of application development, guided like young Hephaestus at the forge of invention. They begin by exploring what apps are, how they are built, and how they solve real problems across every school subject. From planning and designing user interfaces to building their very first working apps, students learn the full 6-step app development process. The term ends with an exciting leap into the Internet of Things (IoT) — where everyday objects communicate through sensors, connectivity, and actuators, like Hermes carrying messages between worlds.

🎯 Learning Outcomes — Term 1 Midterm

  • Define what an application is and identify the four types: mobile, web, desktop, and embedded.
  • Explain how apps are used across different subjects (Math, Science, Language, Social Studies, Health, Art).
  • List and apply the 6 steps of app development: requirements, design, prototype, develop, test, deploy & maintain.
  • Design a clear, accessible user interface (UI) using the 5 core principles: simplicity, consistency, feedback, accessibility, and hierarchy.
  • Apply variables, conditions, and functions to plan and build simple app logic.
  • Define Internet of Things (IoT) and describe how sensors, connectivity, and actuators work together.
  • Identify common sensors (temperature, motion, light, soil moisture) and actuators (LED, buzzer, motor, servo, relay).
  • Design a basic IoT system for a real-world scenario (smart home, smart farm, smart health).

Term 2 — Data Collection, Processing & Evaluation

In this term, students become data detectives, like Thoth weighing the heart against the feather of truth. They learn how to gather information from both primary sources (surveys, interviews, measurements) and secondary sources (government reports, research papers, databases). Students discover how to design fair surveys, sample correctly, and process data using advanced spreadsheet functions. They build evaluation matrices to compare real-world alternatives — like choosing the best phone promotion or the right crop for local soil — and present their findings through clear charts and data storytelling. By the end, every student can make a well-supported, data-driven decision.

🎯 Learning Outcomes — Term 2 Final

  • Distinguish between primary and secondary data and explain when to use each.
  • Design neutral, ethical surveys and apply correct sampling techniques.
  • Calculate and interpret advanced statistics: mean, median, mode, range, and standard deviation.
  • Create and evaluate alternatives using a weighted evaluation matrix.
  • Combine primary and secondary data to recommend a real-world choice (phone promotion, crop selection).
  • Choose the right chart for different data (bar, line, pie, scatter, histogram) and create clear visualizations.
  • Tell a convincing data story with a clear message, charts, and a recommendation.
  • Use advanced spreadsheet functions (IF, VLOOKUP, COUNTIF, SUMIF, pivot tables) and online tools (Datawrapper, Flourish).
  • Evaluate data quality using five criteria: accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, and relevance.
  • Identify bias in data (sampling, response, question, selection) and decide if data is reliable enough for a decision.

Term 3 — Information Reliability & Media Literacy

In today's world, not every message is true. This term teaches students how to navigate information safely, like Odysseus steering between Scylla and Charybdis. They learn to evaluate source reliability, separate fact from opinion, and use the PROMPT method to trace any claim back to its origin. Students sharpen their critical thinking by spotting logical fallacies, analyzing media messages for bias and purpose, and understanding the real-world impact of misinformation. The term closes with students learning how to respond respectfully to false claims and becoming confident, media-literate citizens.

🎯 Learning Outcomes — Term 3 Midterm

  • Define information literacy and distinguish between misinformation, disinformation, fake news, propaganda, and opinion.
  • Evaluate source reliability using five criteria: authority, accuracy, objectivity, currency, and coverage.
  • Apply the PROMPT method (Primary source, Reasoning, Origin, Motive, Proof, Timeliness) to verify information.
  • Distinguish fact, opinion, and belief, and identify signal words that reveal bias.
  • Identify and name common logical fallacies: ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, slippery slope, appeal to emotion, bandwagon, appeal to authority, and hasty generalization.
  • Trace information back to its original source using reverse image search, quote search, and archive tools.
  • Analyze media messages using the framework: purpose, audience, source, techniques, bias, and framing.
  • Explain the individual, social, economic, political, and health impacts of misinformation.
  • Apply respectful response strategies (pause, verify, truth sandwich) to correct misinformation.
  • Create a media analysis report that evaluates reliability, identifies fallacies, and proposes a response.

Term 4 — Safe, Responsible & Legal IT Use

The final term prepares students to become safe, responsible, and law-abiding digital citizens, guided by the wisdom of Athena and the justice of Ma'at. They learn how to shop online safely, avoid scams and phishing, and make smart choices about software, subscriptions, and in-game purchases. Students explore Thai computer laws — including the Computer Crime Act, the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), and the Copyright Act — and learn how fair use allows them to use others' work legally. The term also covers ethical online behaviour: not creating fake news, verifying before sharing, using social media responsibly, and understanding the balance between digital rights and responsibilities. Students finish by designing a real digital citizenship campaign for their school.

🎯 Learning Outcomes — Term 4 Final

  • Identify types of online transactions (e-commerce, digital goods, subscriptions, in-game purchases, bill payments) and their risks.
  • Apply safety practices for online payments: HTTPS, secure methods, never sharing OTPs, keeping receipts.
  • Distinguish licensed, pirated, open-source, freeware, and subscription software, and explain the risks of piracy.
  • Understand subscription terms, auto-renewal, free trials, and how to cancel safely.
  • Analyse in-game purchases (cosmetic, functional, virtual currency, loot boxes, battle passes) and make responsible spending decisions.
  • Identify key Thai computer laws: Computer Crime Act, PDPA, Copyright Act, and their penalties.
  • Apply the four factors of fair use: purpose, nature, amount, and effect on the market.
  • Explain why creating fake news is harmful and commit to not creating or sharing it.
  • Apply a 5-step verification process before sharing: pause, check source, cross-reference, check date, use fact-checking tools.
  • Create a personal social media policy and explain digital rights and responsibilities.
  • Design and present a digital citizenship campaign covering at least 5 unit topics.
