📱 Lesson 5 — Case Study: Choosing a Phone Promotion

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 2 🗓️ Semester 1 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"Three phone promotions. Three different prices. Three different bundles. How do you choose the one that is actually best for you? Not the cheapest — the best."

In this lesson, you will put everything from Lessons 1–4 into practice. You will use primary data (your own usage), secondary data (published rates), an evaluation matrix (weighted criteria), and statistics (to see patterns). The result: a real, evidence-based recommendation that you can defend.

Above them, three small icons: a bar chart, a balance scale, and a checkmark. A cartoon student compares them with a magnifying glass. Warm bronze and parchment tones.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Apply the full data decision process to a real choice.
  • Combine primary and secondary data into a single analysis.
  • Use an evaluation matrix to compare promotions fairly.
  • Write a recommendation report with evidence.
  • Present your recommendation to the class.

📖 1. The Six-Step Decision Process

When making a data-driven decision, follow this six-step process:

  1. Identify your usage pattern (primary data — collect from your own life).
  2. Collect promotion details (secondary data — find from published sources).
  3. Define criteria and weights (what matters most to you?).
  4. Evaluate alternatives (build and use an evaluation matrix).
  5. Choose the best option (highest weighted total, checked with a sensitivity test).
  6. Justify with evidence (write a clear recommendation).
Key idea: This process works for any decision — phone promotions, crops, university programs, sports equipment, travel destinations, or savings plans.

Activity 1 — Plan Your Decision 8 min

In your notebook, answer these questions for a phone promotion decision:

  1. What primary data do you already know about your phone usage? (approximate GB per month, call minutes, budget).
  2. What secondary data would you look for?
  3. List 4 criteria that matter most to you for choosing a promotion.

Share with a partner. Did your criteria match theirs?

📖 2. Case Study: Anan's Decision

A Grade 9 student named Anan wanted to choose a phone promotion. Here is how he walked through the six-step process.

Step 1 — Primary Data (his usage pattern)

  • Monthly data usage: 12 GB
  • Monthly call time: 80 minutes
  • Monthly budget: 400 THB

Step 2 — Secondary Data (three promotions)

Step 3 — Criteria and Weights

  • Price — weight 5 (most important for a student)
  • Data — weight 4 (he needs at least 12 GB)
  • Calls — weight 3 (80 minutes is his average)
  • Contract — weight 2 (flexibility is nice but not essential)

Step 4 — Evaluation Matrix

Step 5 — Choice

Promo A wins. It fits Anan's usage (20 GB > 12 GB needed, 100 min > 80 min needed) and stays under his 400 THB budget.

Step 6 — Justification

"I chose Promo A because it fits my monthly usage (12 GB data, 80 minutes) and my budget (300 THB, under my 400 THB limit). While Promo B has unlimited data, it is over my budget. Promo C is cheaper but has too little data for my needs. My evaluation matrix confirms Promo A is the best fit."

Activity 2 — Analyze Anan's Decision 10 min

Answer in your notebook:

  1. Which promotion was the cheapest? Why did Anan not choose it?
  2. Which promotion gave the most data? Why did Anan not choose it?
  3. If Anan's budget were 500 THB, would his choice change? Show your reasoning.
  4. If Anan only needed 5 GB of data per month, would his choice change?

Discuss: how does knowing your own usage make the decision clearer?

📖 3. Writing a Recommendation Report

A recommendation report explains your choice and the evidence behind it. A good report has five parts:

  1. Decision question: What are you choosing between?
  2. Data used: What primary and secondary data did you collect?
  3. Criteria and weights: What matters, and how much?
  4. Evaluation matrix: Show the scores and totals.
  5. Recommendation: State the winner and explain why with evidence.
Tip: Use numbers and specific details. Instead of "Promo A is better," write "Promo A scored 61 points, compared to 53 for Promo B and 49 for Promo C, because it fits my usage and budget."

Activity 3 — Write Your Recommendation 10 min

Using Anan's case as a model, write a short recommendation (5–7 sentences) for the promotion you would choose if you were Anan.

Include:

  • The promotion you recommend.
  • One sentence describing your primary data.
  • One sentence describing the secondary data.
  • One sentence explaining the weights.
  • The final score and the evidence.

🔍 Case Study — What If Your Usage Is Different?

Anan's classmate, Ploy, has different needs. Her usage pattern is:

  • Monthly data usage: 5 GB
  • Monthly call time: 150 minutes
  • Monthly budget: 500 THB

Now re-evaluate the same three promotions for Ploy:

CriterionWeightPromo APromo BPromo C
Price45 (20)3 (12)5 (20)
Data25 (10)5 (10)5 (10)
Calls55 (25)5 (25)2 (10)
Contract33 (9)5 (15)5 (15)
Total646255

Interesting finding: Promo C now scores much lower because Ploy needs 150 minutes of calls, but Promo C only offers 50.

Reflect: This is why "best" always depends on who is choosing. There is no universal "best phone promotion" — only the best one for a specific user.

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Create Your Recommendation Report

Working in groups, create a full recommendation report for a phone promotion decision using your group's own survey data from Lesson 2.

  1. Decision question: Write one sentence describing the decision.
  2. Data used: Summarize your primary data (survey) and secondary data (provider rates).
  3. Criteria and weights: Show a table with 4 criteria and their weights, justified by your survey results.
  4. Evaluation matrix: Build a matrix with 3 promotions and score them.
  5. Recommendation: Write a 5–7 sentence paragraph recommending the best option with evidence.
  6. Charts: Include at least 2 charts (a bar chart of student usage, a pie chart of preferred features).

When finished, exchange reports with another group. Give each other feedback on clarity and evidence.

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. What are the six steps of the data decision process?
  2. Why did Anan and Ploy choose different promotions?
  3. How does the evaluation matrix make the decision more objective?
  4. What did you learn about your own usage pattern from this lesson?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • The six-step decision process works for any real-world choice.
  • Primary data tells you your needs; secondary data shows available options.
  • Weights reflect what matters most to the user — and different users have different weights.
  • A good recommendation is backed by specific numbers and evidence.
