🚫 Lesson 6 — Not Creating Fake News

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"Have you ever shared something that turned out to be false? Or seen someone create a fake story for attention? Why do people do it? What harm does it cause?"

Creating fake news is not just unkind — it can be illegal and dangerous. In this lesson, you will learn what fake news is, why people create it, what harm it causes, and how to commit to never creating or sharing it.

Image 6.1 — Lesson Opener Description: A warm parchment banner showing a hand holding a pen writing a fake headline on a scroll. A red X hovers over the scroll. In the background, a group of small faces show confusion and worry. Warm bronze tones.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Explain what fake news is and why it is harmful.
  • Identify the motives behind creating fake news.
  • Recognize the types of fake news.
  • Commit to not creating or sharing fake news.

📖 1. What Is Fake News?

Fake news is false or misleading information presented as news. It looks like real news — same format, same style — but the content is not true.

There are five common types.

1) Fabrication

Completely made-up stories. Example: "A celebrity died" — but they are alive.

2) Manipulation

Real information changed to mislead. Example: an old photo with a new caption.

3) Clickbait

Sensational headlines designed to get clicks, but the article does not deliver what the headline promised.

4) Satire

Humor that is not meant to be taken seriously — but sometimes mistaken for real news.

5) Propaganda

Biased information used to promote a political cause or a specific agenda.

Key idea: Fake news is harmful because it can mislead people, cause panic, and damage trust. Creating it is a serious responsibility.

Activity 1 — Identify the Type 8 min

Classify each example as one of the five types.

  1. "You won't believe what happened next!" (but nothing happens).
  2. A fake article saying a politician was arrested.
  3. An old photo of a protest with a new caption.
  4. A humorous site that makes up stories for laughs.
  5. A video that only shows one side of a story to influence opinion.

📖 2. Why People Create Fake News

Understanding motives helps you resist and respond to fake news.

1) Money

Fake news generates clicks and ad revenue. Some websites exist only to make money from sensational headlines.

2) Political Influence

False stories can change how people vote or think about a cause.

3) Attention

Some creators want followers, likes, and shares. Fake news spreads faster than real news, so it brings more attention.

4) Harm to Others

Sometimes the goal is personal — to damage someone's reputation or cause them pain.

5) Mischief

Some people do it "just for fun" without thinking about the consequences.

Tip: When you see a sensational story, ask: "Who benefits if I share this?" The answer often reveals the motive.
Image 6.3 — Five Motives Description: Five parchment cards in a row. Each has a motive and an icon: Money (a coin), Political Influence (a ballot), Attention (a bell), Harm (a broken heart), Mischief (a laughing mask). Warm bronze borders.

Activity 2 — Analyze Fake News 10 min

For each example, write:

  1. What type of fake news is it?
  2. What is the motive?
  3. Who is harmed?
  4. What should be done?

Examples:

  • A fake headline about a celebrity.
  • A manipulated photo.
  • A satirical article that confuses readers.
  • A clickbait title.
  • A political propaganda post.

📖 3. Why It Is Wrong to Create Fake News

Creating fake news causes real harm. Here are four reasons to never do it.

1) It Harms Individuals

People can be hurt, frightened, or defamed by false stories. Sometimes the harm is permanent.

2) It Harms Society

Fake news divides communities, spreads fear, and lowers trust in real journalism.

3) It Is Often Illegal

In Thailand, spreading false computer data is a violation of the Computer Crime Act. Penalties can include fines or imprisonment.

4) It Damages Your Own Reputation

If you are known for creating or sharing fake news, people will stop trusting you. Your credibility is hard to rebuild.

Example: A student created a fake screenshot claiming a teacher said something insulting. The screenshot spread quickly. The teacher was hurt, and the student faced school discipline and potential legal consequences. One fake screenshot caused real damage.
Image 6.4 — Why It Is Wrong Description: Four parchment cards in a 2×2 grid. Each has a reason and an icon: Harm to Individuals (a broken heart), Harm to Society (a divided group), Illegal (a gavel), Reputation (a cracked shield). Warm bronze borders.

Activity 3 — Create a "Stop Fake News" Campaign 10 min

In your group, plan a small campaign to stop fake news. Include:

  1. A slogan (short and memorable).
  2. A poster or short video idea (describe the image or scene).
  3. Key messages (2–3 sentences).
  4. How to verify before sharing (list 3 steps).

Share your campaign with the class. Choose the best slogan to display in the classroom.

🔍 Case Study — The Fake Screenshot

A student created a fake screenshot of a chat where a classmate supposedly said something insulting. He shared it in a group chat "for fun." Within hours, it had been forwarded to dozens of students, and the classmate was being bullied.

What went wrong:

  • The screenshot was completely fabricated.
  • The student who created it did not think about the consequences.
  • The classmate was harmed and humiliated.
  • The student who created the fake screenshot was disciplined by the school and could have faced legal action under the Computer Crime Act.

What should have been done:

  • Never create fake content about others.
  • If you see fake content, report it to a teacher or parent.
  • Support the person being targeted.

Reflect: How did "just for fun" turn into real harm? What is one thing you will never do online after this lesson?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Design a Pledge

Working individually, write a personal pledge to not create or share fake news. Use this template:

"I, ______________, promise to never create or share fake news. I will always pause and check before sharing. I will use the following steps to verify: ______, ______, and ______. I understand that creating fake news is harmful and may be illegal."

Decorate your pledge on a small poster or card. Display your pledge on the classroom wall to remind yourself and your classmates.

Image 6.6 — Pledge Template Description: A parchment pledge template with blank lines and three verification steps. A small hand over a heart icon at the top. Warm parchment tones.

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. Name the five types of fake news.
  2. What are three motives for creating fake news?
  3. Why is creating fake news harmful?
  4. What is your personal pledge to stop fake news?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Fake news comes in five types: fabrication, manipulation, clickbait, satire, propaganda.
  • Common motives: money, political influence, attention, harm, mischief.
  • Creating fake news is harmful and may be illegal.
  • Always pause, verify, and think before sharing.
