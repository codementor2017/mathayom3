🎮 Lesson 3 — In-Game Purchases and Digital Items

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"Have you ever bought a skin, a battle pass, or virtual currency in a game? How much did you spend? Was it worth it? Did you plan to spend that much, or did it just happen?"

In-game purchases are one of the fastest-growing ways to spend money online. They can be fun, but they also use powerful psychological tricks to make you spend more than you intended. In this lesson, you will learn to recognize these tricks and make responsible spending decisions.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Identify the five types of in-game purchases.
  • Recognize the risks and psychological tricks used in games.
  • Make responsible decisions about in-game spending.
  • Create a personal spending plan for games.

📖 1. Five Types of In-Game Purchases

Games offer several ways to spend real money. Knowing them helps you decide.

1) Cosmetic Items

Skins, outfits, emotes, and animations that change how your character looks. They do not affect gameplay.

2) Functional Items

Weapons, power-ups, or boosts that give you an advantage in the game.

3) Virtual Currency

Gems, coins, or V-bucks that you buy with real money and spend inside the game.

4) Loot Boxes

Random rewards — you pay for a box that might contain something rare or something common. Loot boxes work like gambling.

5) Battle Passes

Seasonal rewards that unlock as you play. Usually cheaper than buying individual items.

Key idea: Every in-game purchase costs real money. Virtual items have no real-world value outside the game.

Activity 1 — Classify the Purchase 8 min

Write which type each purchase belongs to.

  1. A new skin for your favorite character.
  2. A pack of 500 gems for 100 THB.
  3. A "mystery box" that contains a random item.
  4. A seasonal pass that unlocks rewards as you play.
  5. A sword that gives +10 damage.
  6. A dance emote for your character.

Compare with a partner. Which purchases feel the most tempting?

📖 2. Risks and Psychological Tricks

Game designers use psychology to encourage spending. Here are the most common tricks.

Limited-Time Offers

"Buy now or lose it forever!" Creates urgency and reduces your time to think.

Fear of Missing Out (FOMO)

You worry you will be left behind if you do not buy. Especially strong in games with competitive seasons.

Social Pressure

Your friends have the skin, so you want it too. This is not a game mechanic — it is real social pressure.

Random Rewards (Loot Boxes)

Like gambling, the randomness keeps you buying. The hope of winning something rare is powerful.

Virtual Currency Confusion

Buying gems or coins hides the real cost. You spend 100 gems, not 100 THB — but 100 gems might cost 100 THB.

Tip: Before you buy anything, ask: "Would I spend this money on something else if I could see the price in real money?" If the answer is no, do not buy.

Activity 2 — Analyze the Offer 10 min

For each in-game offer, write:

  1. Is it worth it?
  2. What psychological tricks are being used?
  3. What is the real cost?
  4. Should you buy it? Why or why not?

Offers:

  • A limited-time skin for 500 THB.
  • A loot box for 100 THB.
  • A battle pass for 300 THB.
  • A "starter pack" for 50 THB.
  • A discount bundle for 1,000 THB.

📖 3. Making Responsible Spending Decisions

You can still enjoy games and buy things you love — but you should do it responsibly.

Set a Monthly Budget

Decide in advance how much you will spend. When the budget is gone, stop.

Wait 24 Hours Before Buying

If you still want something after a day, it is more likely a real desire rather than an impulse.

Convert Virtual Currency to Real Money

Before buying gems or coins, ask: "How much is this in real money?"

Avoid Loot Boxes

Loot boxes are gambling-like. If you enjoy them, set a strict limit. If not, avoid them entirely.

Talk to Your Parents

If you are spending money on games, your parents should know. Never use their card without permission.

Activity 3 — Design a Spending Plan 10 min

In your notebook, write a simple "Responsible In-Game Spending Plan." Include:

  1. My monthly in-game budget: ______ THB.
  2. Rules for buying: (e.g., "I will only buy cosmetics, not loot boxes.")
  3. How I will avoid impulse purchases: (e.g., "I will wait 24 hours.")
  4. How I will recognize gambling-like mechanics.
  5. How I will talk to my parents about spending.

Share your plan with a partner. Give each other feedback on realism and clarity.

🔍 Case Study — The 5,000 THB Battle Pass

A student spent 5,000 THB on in-game purchases over three months without realizing it. He thought he was spending small amounts — "just 50 THB here, 100 THB there."

What happened:

  • He bought a battle pass, a few skins, and 20 loot boxes.
  • Each loot box cost 100 THB, and he kept hoping to win the rare skin.
  • He used his mother's credit card without asking.
  • When the bill arrived, his family discovered the spending.

What was wrong:

  • No budget. Small purchases added up.
  • Loot boxes acted like gambling.
  • He used his mother's card without permission.
  • He did not track his spending.

What should have been done:

  • Set a monthly budget (e.g., 200 THB).
  • Track every purchase in a notebook or app.
  • Avoid loot boxes.
  • Ask before using someone else's card.

Reflect: How could a simple budget have prevented this situation?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Track and Plan

Working individually, complete the following:

  1. Track: Think about the last month. List any in-game purchases you made (or that your friends made). How much did they total?
  2. Analyze: For each purchase, ask:
    • Was it planned or impulse?
    • Did you (or they) feel good about it after?
    • Would you make the same choice again?
  3. Plan: Write a final one-paragraph "Responsible In-Game Spending Plan" for yourself. Include a budget, a rule, and one technique to avoid impulse spending.

When finished, share your plan with a partner. Discuss what you learned.

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. Name the five types of in-game purchases.
  2. What is FOMO, and how does it affect spending?
  3. Why are loot boxes compared to gambling?
  4. What is one rule you will follow from now on?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Five in-game purchase types: cosmetics, functional items, virtual currency, loot boxes, battle passes.
  • Games use limited-time offers, FOMO, social pressure, random rewards, and virtual currency to encourage spending.
  • Set a budget, wait before buying, and track your spending.
  • Loot boxes are gambling-like — approach them with care.

🏠 Homework

Track your in-game spending for one week. Record every purchase (even small ones). At the end of the week, total it up and write a short paragraph (5–7 sentences) reflecting on what you found and whether you would change anything.
