💻 Lesson 2 — Buying Software and Subscriptions Safely

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"A friend offers you a free copy of an expensive program. Another website says the same software costs 100 THB instead of 5,000. Should you say yes? What could go wrong?"

Software is everywhere — apps, games, tools, subscriptions. But not all software is safe, and not all prices are honest. In this lesson, you will learn the difference between licensed, pirated, open-source, and subscription software, and how to make safe choices that protect both you and the people who create the software.

📖 1. Five Types of Software

Software comes in different forms. Knowing the difference is essential.

1) Licensed Software

Paid software with a legal license. You buy it (or subscribe to it) and receive updates and support. Example: Microsoft Office (paid version), Adobe Photoshop.

2) Pirated Software

Illegal copies of paid software. Often distributed through unofficial sites or shared by friends. Pirated software often contains malware and is a common source of computer infections.

3) Open-Source Software

Free software with source code available to the public. You can use, modify, and share it legally. Example: LibreOffice, GIMP, Firefox.

4) Freeware

Free software, but the source code is not shared. It may have limited features or contain ads. Example: Adobe Acrobat Reader.

5) Subscription Software

Software you pay for monthly or yearly. Access ends when you stop paying. Example: Adobe Creative Cloud, Microsoft 365.

Key idea: Legal software protects you and respects the people who made it. Pirated software is not "free" — it often costs you in hidden ways.

📖 2. Risks of Pirated Software

Pirated software may seem like a bargain, but the costs are real.

1) Malware and Viruses

Pirated software often includes hidden malware that can steal your data, lock your files, or spy on you.

2) No Updates or Support

You cannot get security updates, so your computer stays vulnerable to new attacks.

3) Legal Consequences

Using pirated software is illegal in most countries, including Thailand. You can face fines or other penalties.

4) Data Theft

Some pirated software secretly sends your files and passwords to attackers.

5) Crashes and Instability

Pirated software is often modified, which can cause crashes, lost work, and system damage.

Tip: If you need software but cannot afford it, look for a free open-source alternative. There is almost always one available.

📖 3. Subscription Terms — Read Before You Click

Subscriptions are convenient, but they come with terms you must understand.

Read the Terms

Before subscribing, read the "Terms of Service." Look for the price, the renewal date, and the cancellation policy.

Check Auto-Renewal

Many subscriptions renew automatically. If you do not cancel before the renewal date, you will be charged again.

Know How to Cancel

Before subscribing, find out how to cancel. If it is difficult to find, that is a warning sign.

Watch Free Trials

Free trials often require a payment method. If you forget to cancel, you will be charged when the trial ends.

Example: A streaming service offers a 30-day free trial. If you do not cancel by day 29, you will be charged 350 THB on day 30. Set a reminder to cancel before the trial ends.

🔍 Case Study — The 100 THB Photoshop Scam

A student found a website selling Adobe Photoshop for 100 THB — the real price is thousands of baht. The website promised "lifetime access, full features, instant download."

What happened:

  • The student paid 100 THB via bank transfer.
  • The download link was a pirated version of Photoshop.
  • The software contained hidden malware.
  • Within a week, the student's laptop was slow, and one of their accounts was hacked.

What was wrong:

  • The price was too good to be true.
  • The website was not the official Adobe site.
  • Payment was via bank transfer (no protection).
  • The download was pirated software — illegal and dangerous.

What should have been done:

  • Use free open-source alternatives like GIMP.
  • Or subscribe to Adobe's official plan (student discounts available).
  • Never download software from unofficial sources.

Reflect: Why is "too good to be true" a good rule of thumb for software?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Software comes in five forms: licensed, pirated, open-source, freeware, subscription.
