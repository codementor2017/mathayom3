📚 Lesson 5 — Fair Use of Copyright

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"A student uses a copyrighted song in a school project. Is that allowed? A YouTuber uses a short clip in a commentary video. Is that allowed? Fair use says: sometimes yes, but with conditions."

Copyright protects creators, but it also has limits. The doctrine of fair use allows limited use of copyrighted material for education, commentary, news, parody, and other purposes. In this lesson, you will learn the four factors of fair use and how to apply them to real situations.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Define fair use and its purpose.
  • Apply the four factors of fair use.
  • Distinguish between fair use and copyright infringement.
  • Make safe, ethical choices when using others' work.

📖 1. What Is Fair Use?

Fair use is a legal exception that allows limited use of copyrighted material without the owner's permission. It exists to balance two goals:

  • Protecting the rights of creators.
  • Allowing the public to use small portions for education, commentary, and creativity.

Fair use is not a "free pass." It has rules. When in doubt, ask the four questions you will learn next.

Key idea: Fair use is a defense, not a permission slip. It applies only when the four factors weigh in your favor.

📖 2. The Four Factors of Fair Use

Courts use four factors to decide whether a use is fair. All four matter.

1) Purpose

Why are you using it? Educational, non-profit, and transformative uses (like commentary or parody) are more likely to be fair. Commercial use is less likely.

2) Nature of the Work

What kind of work is it? Factual works (like news) are more likely to be fair to use than highly creative works (like songs or novels).

3) Amount Used

How much did you use? A small portion is more likely to be fair than the whole work. The key is whether you used the heart of the work.

4) Effect on the Market

Does your use replace the market for the original? If people would have bought the original but now get it from you, it is less likely to be fair.

Example — school essay: Quoting one paragraph of a book for a school report.
  • Purpose: educational ✓
  • Nature: creative work (book) — mixed
  • Amount: one paragraph — small ✓
  • Effect: no impact on the market ✓
Likely fair use.

📖 3. Examples of Fair Use and Infringement

Examples of likely fair use:

  • Quoting a paragraph in a school essay.
  • Using a short clip in a commentary or review video.
  • Parody or satire.
  • News reporting that shows a small clip or image.
  • Educational use in a classroom setting.

Examples of likely infringement:

  • Copying a whole textbook and giving it away.
  • Using a song in a commercial video without a license.
  • Selling someone else's artwork as your own.
  • Using an entire chapter of a book in a public slideshow.
  • Streaming a full movie on a public platform.
Tip: When in doubt, look for Creative Commons licensed material or public domain works. These are free to use with conditions or restrictions.

🔍 Case Study — The YouTuber and the Song

A YouTuber wanted to use a popular song in a video showing highlights of a sports game. The video was monetized (ads ran, and the creator earned money from views). The YouTuber used 30 seconds of a 3-minute song and claimed fair use.

Applying the four factors:

  • Purpose: Commercial (monetized) — weighs against fair use.
  • Nature: Music is highly creative — weighs against fair use.
  • Amount: 30 seconds — a significant portion of the song.
  • Effect: The use could replace licensing fees — weighs against fair use.

Result: The YouTuber's video was demonetized, and the copyright owner could have taken further action. The claim of fair use did not hold.

Reflect: Why did this use fail the fair use test? What could the YouTuber have done differently?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Fair Use Analysis

Working in pairs, analyze three cases of using copyrighted material.

  1. A student video project that uses a 10-second clip of a movie.
  2. A school newsletter that reprints a photo without permission.
  3. A social media post that uses a famous artwork as a background image.

For each case, apply the four factors:

  • Purpose (educational, non-profit, transformative, commercial)
  • Nature (factual or creative)
  • Amount (small or large portion)
  • Effect (impact on the market for the original)

Write a one-paragraph verdict for each case, explaining your reasoning.

Present your analysis to the class. Discuss which case was the trickiest.

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. What is fair use, and why does it exist?
  2. Name the four factors of fair use.
  3. Give an example of fair use and an example of infringement.
  4. What is one way you can ethically use others' work?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Fair use allows limited use of copyrighted material without permission.
  • Four factors: Purpose, Nature, Amount, Effect on the market.
  • All four factors matter — even one against fair use can change the outcome.
  • Safe alternatives: Creative Commons, public domain, or asking for permission.
