🏆 Lesson 10 — Final Project: Data-Driven Decision

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 2 🗓️ Semester 1 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"You have learned how to collect data, process it, evaluate alternatives, visualize findings, and check reliability. Now it is time to put it all together — one complete data-driven decision, from start to finish."

In this final lesson for Indicator 2, you will design and present a full data-driven decision. You will choose a real decision, collect primary and secondary data, process it with advanced tools, evaluate alternatives, and present a recommendation backed by evidence.

Image 10.1 — Lesson Opener Description: A warm parchment banner showing a large golden laurel wreath around a tablet that displays a data dashboard with charts and tables. On either side, small clay tablets show the steps of the data process. A cartoon student stands proudly beside the project. Warm gold and bronze tones.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Apply the full data process from question to recommendation.
  • Combine primary and secondary data in one project.
  • Use advanced tools (pivot tables, Datawrapper) to process and visualize.
  • Present a data-driven recommendation with evidence.
  • Reflect on what you learned and how you would improve.

📖 1. Your Project Task

Your task is to make a real data-driven decision. You will choose a decision, collect and process data, evaluate alternatives, and present a recommendation.

Your project must include:

  1. A clear decision question.
  2. Primary data collected from at least 15 respondents.
  3. Secondary data from at least 2 reliable sources.
  4. Data processing using advanced functions (mean, median, mode, range, SD).
  5. At least 2 charts created in Google Sheets, Datawrapper, or Flourish.
  6. At least 3 alternatives evaluated in an evaluation matrix.
  7. A recommendation with evidence.
  8. A 5-minute presentation to the class.
  9. A short written reflection.
Tip: Choose a decision that matters to you or your community. Good projects come from real questions, not textbook exercises.
Image 10.2 — Project Options Description: A parchment grid with six panels showing different decision projects: choosing a phone promotion, a crop for local soil, a university program, a fitness routine, a laptop for school, or a travel destination. Each panel has a small icon and label. Warm bronze frames.

Activity 1 — Choose Your Decision 10 min

In your notebook, write down your choice for the following:

  1. Decision question: What are you choosing between?
  2. Primary data: What will you collect, and from whom?
  3. Secondary data: What sources will you use?
  4. Why it matters: One sentence explaining why this decision is important.

Get your plan approved by your teacher before starting.

📖 2. Project Planning

Before you begin, write a full plan. Use this structure:

Step 1 — Decision Question

Write one clear sentence describing the decision. Example: "Which phone promotion is best for a Grade 9 student with a 400 THB monthly budget?"

Step 2 — Primary Data Collection

Design a short survey (5–8 questions). Collect at least 15 responses using a Google Form. Ensure the survey is anonymous and voluntary.

Step 3 — Secondary Data Collection

Find at least 2 reliable sources. Examples: government reports, official provider websites, university research.

Step 4 — Data Processing

Use Google Sheets to calculate:

  • Mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation of your numeric data.
  • Frequency tables for categorical questions.
  • At least 2 charts.

Step 5 — Alternatives and Evaluation

List at least 3 alternatives. Build an evaluation matrix with 4 criteria and weights.

Step 6 — Recommendation and Reflection

Write your recommendation with evidence. Then write a reflection paragraph (6–8 sentences).

Image 10.3 — Project Plan Template Description: A parchment template with six labeled boxes stacked vertically: Decision Question, Primary Data, Secondary Data, Data Processing, Alternatives, Recommendation. Each box has a small icon and blank lines for writing. A quill and ink pot at the bottom right. Warm parchment tones.

Activity 2 — Write Your Plan 15 min

In your notebook, complete the six planning steps. Be specific.

Show your plan to your teacher before moving on to the build phase.

📖 3. Building Your Project

Now it is time to build. Follow your plan step by step.

Recommended workflow:

  1. Create your Google Form and share it with at least 15 respondents.
  2. While responses come in, find your secondary data sources.
  3. Once you have responses, open Google Sheets and process the data.
  4. Build your evaluation matrix.
  5. Create your charts.
  6. Write your recommendation and reflection.
  7. Prepare your 5-minute presentation.

Keep your plan on your desk. Refer to it often. If you get stuck, ask your teacher or a classmate for help — but try your own solution first.

Time check: You have about 30 minutes to work on your project in class. Focus on getting the core features done before polishing.
Image 10.4 — Building Your Project Description: A cartoon student at a desk with a laptop showing a Google Sheet and a Google Form. On the desk: a notebook with survey questions, a calculator, and a sticky note that says "Process → Evaluate → Recommend." Warm bronze tones.

Activity 3 — Test Your Process 10 min

Before presenting, test your work:

  1. Are your statistics correct? Re-calculate one value by hand to check.
  2. Is your evaluation matrix complete? Add up the totals again.
  3. Are your charts clear? Do they have titles, labels, and source notes?
  4. Does your recommendation use evidence from your data?

If anything is missing, fix it before presenting.

🔍 Case Study — A Decision That Made a Difference

Last year, a Grade 9 group chose to answer the question: "Which study method is best for our class before exams?"

Primary data: They surveyed 45 classmates about study habits, hours, and preferred methods (reading, flashcards, group study, practice tests).

Secondary data: They found two research papers on study effectiveness from education journals.

Processing: Using Google Sheets, they calculated average hours, median test scores, and created two charts.

Evaluation matrix: They scored four study methods against four criteria: effectiveness, time required, enjoyment, and materials needed.

Recommendation: Practice tests won with the highest total. The group presented to the class, showing evidence from both their survey and the research.

Result: Several students switched their study method, and the class average on the next exam rose by 8%.

Reflect: What made this project strong? (Hint: the decision mattered to the group's real life.)

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Prepare Your Presentation

You have about 15 minutes to prepare a 5-minute presentation. Your presentation must include:

  1. Decision question — one sentence.
  2. Primary data — survey method, sample size.
  3. Secondary data — sources used.
  4. Processing and charts — show at least 2 charts.
  5. Alternatives and evaluation matrix — show the matrix.
  6. Recommendation — with evidence.
  7. What you learned — one sentence.

Practice with a partner. Give each other feedback on clarity and time.

Image 10.6 — Presentation Setup Description: A cartoon student practicing a presentation in front of a partner. A tablet shows a slide with a data dashboard: charts, matrix, and recommendation. Warm parchment tones.

📝 Presentation & Reflection

Each group presents for 5 minutes. After all presentations, write a short reflection in your notebook:

  1. What was the hardest part of your project?
  2. How did the data help you make a better decision?
  3. What would you improve if you had one more week?
  4. What did you learn from watching other groups' presentations?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • The full data process moves from question to recommendation.
  • Good projects use both primary and secondary data.
  • Advanced tools (pivot tables, Datawrapper) make processing faster.
  • Every recommendation should be backed by specific evidence.

🏠 Homework

Write a reflection paragraph (6–8 sentences) answering:

"How did the data process help me make a better decision? What is one thing I am proud of, and one thing I would do differently next time?"

Submit your full project documentation (plan, data, matrix, charts, recommendation, reflection) at the start of the next lesson.
