📖 1. What Is Mitosis?

Mitosis is the process by which one cell divides into two identical cells. It is how living things grow, heal, and replace old cells. Mitosis has four main stages, often remembered as PMAT:

  • Prophase: the chromosomes condense and become visible. The nuclear membrane begins to break down.
  • Metaphase: the chromosomes line up along the middle of the cell.
  • Anaphase: the chromosomes separate and move to opposite ends.
  • Telophase: two new nuclei form; the cell begins to split into two.
Key fact: In humans, each cell starts with 46 chromosomes. After mitosis, each of the two new cells still has 46 chromosomes. Mitosis produces identical copies — no information is lost.

📖 2. Using a Loop to Simulate Stages

A loop is a programming tool that repeats a set of instructions. Since mitosis has four stages that happen in order, a loop is a perfect fit: we can store the stages in a list and let the loop go through them one at a time.

Here is the basic idea in Python:

stages = ["Prophase", "Metaphase", "Anaphase", "Telophase"]

for i, stage in enumerate(stages):
    print(f"Stage {i+1}: {stage}")
    print(f"  Chromosomes are doing their job in {stage.lower()}...")

print("Cell division complete. Two new cells formed.")

Let's break down what happens:

  • List: stages holds the four stage names.
  • Loop: for goes through each stage one at a time.
  • enumerate: gives us both the position (0, 1, 2, 3) and the stage name.
  • f-string: formats the output nicely.
Output of the program:
Stage 1: Prophase
Stage 2: Metaphase
Stage 3: Anaphase
Stage 4: Telophase
Cell division complete. Two new cells formed.

📖 3. Adding Details to the Simulation

A basic simulation is fine, but we can make it much more informative by adding detail: a description for each stage, and the number of chromosomes. 

stages = [
    ("Prophase", "Chromosomes condense and become visible."),
    ("Metaphase", "Chromosomes line up at the center."),
    ("Anaphase", "Chromosomes separate and move to opposite poles."),
    ("Telophase", "Two new nuclei form.")
]

chromosomes = 46

for i, (stage, description) in enumerate(stages):
    print(f"Stage {i+1}: {stage}")
    print(f"  {description}")
    print(f"  Chromosomes: {chromosomes}")

print(f"Result: 2 cells, each with {chromosomes} chromosomes.")

Now the simulation teaches something real — the science is visible.

Tip: When you build a simulation, always ask: "What would help the user understand the concept better?" Sometimes a simple description or number makes all the difference.

🔍 Case Study — Comparing Mitosis and Meiosis

Mitosis produces two identical cells. But there is another kind of cell division called meiosis — which produces four different cells, each with half the chromosomes. Meiosis is how sperm and egg cells are made.

Here is a quick comparison:

Feature Mitosis Meiosis
Number of cells produced24
Chromosomes per cell46 (same)23 (half)
Identical or different?IdenticalDifferent
Where it happensAll body cellsReproductive organs
PurposeGrowth and repairMaking sperm and egg cells
