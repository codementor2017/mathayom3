⚖️ Lesson 9 — Digital Rights and Responsibilities

⏱️ 100 minutes 📘 Indicator 4 🗓️ Semester 2 · Final 🎓 Grade 9

🧭 Lesson Opener

"You have the right to express your opinion online. But do you have the right to say anything you want, about anyone, at any time? Every right comes with a responsibility."

In this lesson, you will learn about your digital rights and the responsibilities that come with them. You will analyze real scenarios and create a class charter that describes what it means to be a responsible digital citizen.

Image 9.1 — Lesson Opener Description: A warm parchment banner showing a balance scale. On one side: "Rights" with icons of a speech bubble, a lock, and a book. On the other side: "Responsibilities" with icons of a heart, a handshake, and a shield. The scale is balanced. Warm bronze tones.

🎯 What You Will Learn

  • Explain your digital rights.
  • Explain the responsibilities that come with them.
  • Analyze scenarios that balance rights and responsibilities.
  • Create a Digital Rights and Responsibilities Charter for your class.

📖 1. Your Digital Rights

Digital rights are the rights you have when you use the internet and digital tools.

1) Right to Privacy

You have the right to control who sees your personal data. This is protected by the PDPA.

2) Right to Freedom of Expression

You have the right to share your ideas and opinions — within the limits of the law.

3) Right to Access Information

You have the right to seek and receive information from the internet.

4) Right to Intellectual Property

You have the right to own the things you create — writing, art, code, music.

5) Right to Safety

You have the right to be protected from harm, harassment, and exploitation online.

Key idea: Rights are not unlimited. They exist alongside the rights of others — and they come with responsibilities.

Activity 1 — Match the Right 8 min

Match each scenario to the digital right involved.

  1. Posting your opinion about a school policy.
  2. Asking a website to delete your data.
  3. Searching for information about a university.
  4. Owning the photos you took.
  5. Reporting someone who is harassing you online.

📖 2. The Responsibilities That Come With Rights

Every right has a matching responsibility. Here are the five pairs.

Right Matching Responsibility
Privacy Respect others' privacy — do not share their data without consent.
Freedom of Expression Do not use your voice to harm, defame, or mislead others.
Access to Information Verify information before using or sharing it.
Intellectual Property Respect others' copyright — do not copy or steal their work.
Safety Do not harm others online; report harmful content.
Tip: When you exercise a right, ask yourself: "Does this respect the rights of others?" If yes, you are on solid ground. If no, reconsider.
Image 9.3 — Rights and Responsibilities Description: A parchment table with two columns (Right, Responsibility) and five rows. Each row is connected by a small arrow. A small balance scale sits above the table. Warm bronze tones.

Activity 2 — Analyze the Scenario 10 min

For each scenario, identify the right and the responsibility involved.

  1. Posting an opinion about a political issue.
  2. Sharing someone's photo without permission.
  3. Downloading a movie for free.
  4. Reporting a harmful post.
  5. Using someone else's work in a project without credit.

Discuss: was each action balanced (right respected and responsibility honored)?

📖 3. Balancing Rights and Responsibilities

Sometimes rights conflict. Here is how to think through a conflict.

Example: Freedom of Expression vs. Right to Safety

You have the right to express your opinion. But if your opinion directly harms someone else — for example, revealing private information about them — the other person's right to safety comes first.

Example: Right to Access vs. Copyright

You have the right to access information. But if you access it by downloading pirated software or media, you violate copyright. Your right does not cancel someone else's.

When rights conflict, the guiding principle is respect: your rights end where another person's rights begin.

Image 9.4 — Balancing Rights Description: A parchment showing two people pulling on a rope, each holding a scroll labeled with a right. Between them, a small balance scale tips back and forth. Warm bronze tones.

Activity 3 — Create a Digital Rights Charter 10 min

In your group, create a "Digital Rights and Responsibilities Charter" for your school.

Include:

  1. 5 rights every student should have online.
  2. 5 matching responsibilities.
  3. How to balance them when they conflict.
  4. How to resolve conflicts (e.g., talk to a teacher, report harmful content).

Present your charter to the class. Combine the best ideas into a class charter.

🔍 Case Study — The Photo Without Permission

A student named Mai took a funny photo of her friend at lunch. Without asking, she posted it on her public account. Within hours, the photo was being shared and commented on by dozens of people, some of whom were making fun of her friend.

Rights involved:

  • Mai: freedom of expression — she can share what she creates.
  • Her friend: privacy — she has the right to control images of herself.

Responsibility: Mai had a responsibility to respect her friend's privacy. She should have asked for permission before posting.

Outcome: Mai's friend was upset and asked her to delete the post. Mai apologized and removed it. But the photo had already been shared by others.

Reflect: How could this situation have been avoided with a single question?

🛠️ Hands-On Task — Class Digital Charter

Your class will work together to create a joint "Digital Rights and Responsibilities Charter."

  1. Each group presents its charter from Activity 3 (4 minutes).
  2. The class votes on the best 5 rights and 5 responsibilities.
  3. Write the final charter on a large poster.
  4. Every student signs the charter.
  5. Hang the charter in the classroom.

The charter is a class commitment. Refer to it when discussing any online incident.

Image 9.6 — Class Charter Poster Description: A large parchment poster with the title "Digital Rights & Responsibilities" at the top. Two columns: Rights on the left, Responsibilities on the right. Lines at the bottom for student signatures. Warm parchment tones.

📝 Reflection & Review

  1. Name three digital rights and their matching responsibilities.
  2. What does it mean to balance rights and responsibilities?
  3. How did your group's charter reflect your class's values?
  4. What is one right you will exercise more responsibly from now on?

🔑 Key Takeaways

  • Digital rights: privacy, expression, access, intellectual property, safety.
  • Every right comes with a matching responsibility.
  • Your rights end where another person's rights begin.
  • A class charter makes digital responsibility visible and shared.
